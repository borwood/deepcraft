# Things that will happen in this game

A one-pager of concrete, informative examples — **the mental model, not the
mechanisms.** Created 2026-07-20 at the user's instruction: *"probably we
need one-pager to append one-line 'things that will happen in this game'
whenever a genuinely informative new example pops up, so new sessions can
start with the right mental model."*

**Rules for this file.** One line per example. Append only. Add a line the
moment a genuinely *informative* example surfaces in conversation — one
that would change how a reader imagines the game, not merely restate a
feature. Read this file at the start of a session, before the design docs;
it is the fastest way to load what this game actually is. Not a promise
list and not a roadmap — some of these are far off, and that is fine.

---

- You dig 200 m down, hit a wall that erosion refused to cut, and the strata
  *above* it tell you how long it has been there.
- You mine a coal seam that exists because a swamp sat at base level, quietly,
  for millions of years — and the rock above it records the flood that ended it.
- You breach the floor of a lake into a cave below, and the lake drains into
  it. Permanently. The basin is dry from then on.
- You dig a channel a kilometre from a river and water runs to the far end,
  because it is the same body of water, not a copy of it.
- You find a cave that used to be underwater: dissolved smooth below the old
  water table, dry now, growing flowstone from the drips still coming through.
- You read a cliff face and know the sea came and went, because the layers say
  so and nobody wrote them.
- The sun low on the horizon shines into a cave mouth, through a window, under
  an overhang — and the simulation knows it does, not just the shader.
- Your torch's reach depends on what you made it out of. A better material
  lights more of the room.
- The dark is dangerous, and light is equipment you carry, maintain and run
  out of.
- Caves have their own ecology, because ecology is a diagnosis of conditions
  and "no light, stable temperature, wet, fed from outside" is a set of
  conditions like any other.
- A creature sees a band of light you cannot, and behaves accordingly.
- A modder adds a second moon on a different axis, and the world's nights,
  tides and night-blooming things all change without the modder touching any
  of them.
- Ancient aliens built impenetrable outposts in geological prehistory, and the
  whole downstream simulation takes it seriously — burial, exhumation, the lot.
- The sun's arc across your sky depends on where you are standing, so poles
  and tropics are somewhere you travel to, not a setting anyone chose.
- You pan gold flakes from a gravel bar. The vein they eroded out of is
  somewhere upstream — so you read the river, and walk upstream.
- A coal seam dead-ends at a fault. The white ash stripe forty meters up —
  the same afternoon, four million years ago, everywhere it appears — is
  offset by the same throw. Follow it, find your seam again.
- A land can be a desert in every way that matters to its rocks and its life,
  yet look green from a hill — because "arid" is a verdict about water
  balance, not a colour, and the two only sometimes agree.
